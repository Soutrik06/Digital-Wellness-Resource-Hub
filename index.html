Browser Productivity Extension

Eliminating YouTube Algorithmic Traps Using Unhook

Setup Time: 3 mins

YouTube is indispensable for lecture recordings, programming tutorials, and homework walk-throughs. However, the homepage recommendations, sidebar video wormholes, comments section, and vertical Shorts are specifically engineered to hijack student attention. This guide details how to configure Unhook to strip all algorithmic engagement loops.

Unhook Extension Menu — Chrome / Firefox / Edge
Hide Home Feed Replaces home feed with zen search bar
Hide Video Sidebar Recommendations Prevents side-video autoplay rabbit holes
Hide Shorts Tab & Vertical Carousels Eliminates algorithmic vertical doomscrolling
Hide Comments & Chat Replay Removes cognitive noise and flame wars
1

Install Unhook from Official Web Stores

Visit the Chrome Web Store, Firefox Add-ons, or Microsoft Edge Add-ons repository. Search for Unhook - Remove YouTube Recommended Videos. Verify the author is Unhook App and click Add Extension. It requires zero accounts and zero sign-in credentials.

2

Configure Minimalist Academic Preset

Navigate to `youtube.com`. Click the puzzle icon in your browser toolbar, pin the Unhook icon, and click it. Ensure the top master toggle is set to ON. Check the boxes for Hide Home Feed, Hide Video Sidebar, Hide Comments, and Hide Shorts.

3

Test the Academic Result

Refresh YouTube. The home screen will now display only an empty, calm search bar. Type the topic of your college lecture (e.g. "Linear Algebra 18.06 MIT"). When you open a lecture video, the sidebar and comment clutter are completely absent. When the lecture finishes, you exit cleanly without algorithmic rabbit holes.

Q: Does this block YouTube videos on my smartphone app?

A: No. Unhook is a browser extension and operates solely inside Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge desktop/mobile browser tabs. For native mobile apps, use bedtime grayscale or app timers.

Q: What if I need to find related lecture clips?

A: You can click the Unhook toolbar icon at any time and uncheck "Hide Video Sidebar" temporarily, or use the top search bar with specific playlist queries.

Mobile & Circadian Sleep Hygiene

Configuring Scheduled Grayscale & Bedtime Sleep Focus

Setup Time: 5 mins

Bright saturated colors on social media trigger dopamine anticipation in the brain, turning brief bedtime checks into hour-long doomscrolling sessions. Converting your mobile screen to monochrome black-and-white after 10:30 PM drastically reduces psychological allure and promotes natural sleep onset.

iPhone / iPad (iOS) Setup

  1. Open Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size.
  2. Tap Color Filters, toggle ON, and choose Grayscale.
  3. To enable quick toggles: Navigate to Accessibility Shortcut at the bottom of Accessibility settings, and select Color Filters. Now triple-clicking the side power button instantly switches your screen between full color and monochrome.
  4. Scheduled Automation: Open the built-in Shortcuts app, tap Automation > New Automation, select Time of Day (e.g. 10:30 PM), add action Set Color Filters ON, and create a morning trigger at 7:00 AM to switch it OFF.

Android (Samsung / Pixel / Motorola) Setup

  1. Open Settings > Digital Wellbeing & Parental Controls.
  2. Tap Bedtime mode (built natively into Android 10+).
  3. Tap Turn on based on a schedule and specify your target study wind-down hours (e.g. 10:30 PM – 7:00 AM).
  4. Under Screen options at bedtime, check Grayscale and Keep the screen dark.
  5. Configure Do Not Disturb to allow calls from favorite emergency contacts while silencing group chats and promotional notifications.
Q: What if I have an emergency call from family at night?

A: In both iOS Focus and Android Bedtime Mode, configure the VIP exception whitelist: add your parents, roommates, or campus safety to 'Allowed Callers'. Repeated calls within 3 minutes also automatically break through DND by default.

Q: Does grayscale make reading PDF textbooks hard?

A: Plain-text reading is unaffected. If you need to view color charts, use the triple-click side button shortcut on iPhone or Quick Settings tile on Android to momentarily pause grayscale.

Open-Source & Local-First Telemetry

Self-Hosted Screen Time Auditing with ActivityWatch

Setup Time: 6 mins

Commercial productivity trackers like RescueTime transmit your private window titles, document filenames, and URL browsing logs to remote cloud corporate servers. ActivityWatch is 100% free and open-source (FOSS): all screen time metrics are stored locally on your own machine at `localhost:5600`.

ActivityWatch Local Dashboard — http://localhost:5600
VS Code / Programming 2h 45m (Productive)
Research PDF (Canvas Portal) 1h 12m (Productive)
Reddit & Social Feeds 38m (Leisure)
1

Download the Official Executable

Head to activitywatch.net and download the release for Windows, macOS, or Linux. Unzip the archive and run `aw-qt`. An ActivityWatch tray icon will appear in your system notification area.

2

Install the Browser Watcher Extension (Critical for Tab Granularity)

By default, ActivityWatch only logs desktop process names (e.g. `chrome.exe`). To see whether you were reading lecture slides or watching entertainment videos, install the official aw-watcher-web extension from the Chrome or Firefox store. It connects solely to `http://localhost:5600`.

3

Review Your Daily Timeline in the Local Dashboard

Right-click the system tray icon and click Open Dashboard. You will be greeted with an interactive visual breakdown of active vs AFK (away-from-keyboard) time, category distribution, and top applications without third-party tracking.

Q: Does ActivityWatch transmit data over the Internet?

A: No. ActivityWatch has zero cloud database servers. If you disconnect your computer from Wi-Fi completely, the dashboard and logging continue to run normally.

Q: Does it slow down my computer?

A: ActivityWatch is lightweight and consumes under 40 MB of RAM, significantly less than typical browser tabs.
